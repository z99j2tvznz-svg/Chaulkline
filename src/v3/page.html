New in V3.25

  • Coaching test, the zone the way you asked: the white line is the ideal line for this ball to get shape on the next one. Green is the right side of it, with more angle on the next ball (a little harder to pot, the cue ball moves naturally). Yellow is the other side, less angle toward straight in: still workable, but it takes more stroke. Past those, it's a different shot. The white ring is still where this stroke sends the cue ball.
  • After each coached shot, big enough to read from the TV: where the cue ball ended (in the green, in the yellow, outside the zone, or the miss), then what your stroke did against the plan's: overcut or undercut on a miss, too hard or too soft (with the speed it really was), not enough draw or too much follow. It's worked out from where the balls went and when they were seen there, in the same physics the plan uses. When it can't tell, it says nothing rather than guess.
  • A planned ball that goes in late now counts for the shot's position too (on Nov 17 five of them first said "outside the zone").

New in V3.24

  • V3.24.4: your five-ball check from 6 spots found the last big error: every ball read a little short of its cushion, on every rail, because the outline the app finds sits about ½ in outside the cushions. That's now allowed for, and balls are read at the height that goes with it. Your 6 spots read twice as close to true (about ¼ in, was over ½ in). More ball checks keep tuning both. The 3 can stay where it is (you set it at the 2nd diamond; it's found there now).
  • V3.24.3: the table check's balls stay put. Set five numbered balls once, against the cushions at diamonds (the list is in the check), and leave them for every spot: from wherever the phone is, the app knows which ball is which and which side it's on. Capture the balls reads them where they are; they need not be exactly on the rings.
  • V3.24.2: your table check from 7 spots worked: every spot agrees this table's diamonds sit about 2½ in out from the outline and 1½ in above the cloth, and the app now fits with that. Ball positions are read to match (V3.24.0's reading height went with the old, too-low diamond height and would have been 2 in off with the new one).
  • V3.24.1: drill and exam results as a big picture you can read from the TV: a green check with sparkles for a make, a red X for a miss, a yellow question mark when the app can't tell (shoot that one again). And the exam's scoring checked against your Nov 16 corrections: the made stop shots that were set aside count, and a real miss stays a miss.
  • Table check (button above): set the phone, find the table and tap Use this spot. The outline is fine-tuned on the rail diamonds and you see how well each rail sits and where the phone is. Optional: freeze four balls against the cushions at the rings to see how true ball positions read; that spot's reading is corrected at once. Save the spot, move or turn the phone, Next spot. With two or more spots, Put the spots together works out this table's diamonds for every session after.
  • Ball positions are truer: they had read 1 to 3½ inches toward the phone, most at the far rail (the four rail balls of BU F7 on Nov 16 read up to 3.4 in off, two of them on or past the near cushion). Now within about half an inch. This is also why the drill and exam marks seemed hard to hit.
  • BU exam: firm stop shots on F2 and F4 count now (the ball was in before the camera saw the shot start, so three made shots were set aside); a stop is measured from where the cue ball really started; a quick miss call no longer comes from a track that jumped across the table; F7 waits for its rail ball to be seen on its mark.
  • "Not a ball" on a drill or exam mark no longer stops a ball being seen there afterwards.

New in V3.23

  • Cut ladder in rounds: 10°, 20°, 30°, 40° and 50° cuts, three shots at each, a diamond from the pocket; then the same ladder 2, 3, 4 and 5 diamonds out. Pick where to start: easy (1 diamond out) to expert (5). The cue ball moves a little from step to step.
  • More room setting up: a ball counts as on its X within about 2½ inches. In BU drills F2, F3 and F4 (down the rail) only the place along the rail counts: set each ball anywhere on the faint line through its X, from the rail out to a diamond, and line the shot up straight yourself (the rules allow it). The marks now sit on a line that goes in.
  • Faster drills: a shot is scored the moment the ball drops when only the pot counts (BU F1 and F6, Cut ladder, Thin cuts, Bank), so you can grab the cue ball while it's still rolling and set up the next one. Scratches don't count against drill shots (except F6, by BU's rules).
  • BU scoring fixes: a ball seen going in late is no longer called "wrong pocket" (Nov 16: F5 shot 7 and F6 shot 4 were); the exam runs on practice rules whatever screen you start it from; a little nudge of the cue ball is not a shot.
  • The cue ball's trail on hard shots: where the camera couldn't keep up, the trail now goes the way the ball must have gone, off the ball it hit and the cushions, instead of straight to where it was next found.

New in V3.22

  • BU Exam I (Menu → Drills): Billiard University's eight fundamentals drills, laid out on the table and scored by their rules, with the score sheet and your Exam II level at the end. "Change last" at the bottom fixes any shot the camera got wrong.
  • Drills: each ball goes on an X on the cloth (touch it with a wet finger and set the ball on the mark); a mark only goes green with the ball within about an inch; the result gives the real cut when the balls were set off; no more "nothing in" before a late call; a ball called in late from its spot counts.
  • Pop-ups fit the screen and scroll, upright or sideways.
  • Coaching: the thin/thick band is gone. You see the aim line, the object ball's line, the cue ball's line, and any other ball the stroke will hit with its path.
  • The plan plays the simplest stroke that does the job (stun, roll, a little draw) and only adds spin, power or cushions when the simple one can't get position. Hard hits and banks or kicks are rated harder to make.
  • A ball hanging in the pocket is planned as a pot (it was taken as already in).

New in V3.21

  • Aim trainer (button above): practise aiming anywhere. The shot is seen from where your eyes are when you are down on it; drag to swing the cue, use the strip at the bottom for fine aim, and shoot.
  • After each shot: the ghost ball you should have hit, your aim beside it, how many degrees off you were and whether it was thin or thick, and how much room the shot gave you.
  • Pick the cut to work on (straight, three-quarter, half ball, quarter ball, sliver) and the pockets (Pro, Amateur, Bar box). Stats by cut show which cuts you miss and on which side.

New in V3.20

  • The coaching rules now come from published pool instruction (Dr. Dave Alciatore, Tom Ross), not guesses: keep it simple (stop shots and natural roll first; side spin, draw, extra cushions and long cue ball travel only when they get better shape), come into the line instead of crossing it, leave an angle, stay off the cushions, slower speed for a bigger pocket.
  • No fixed "best distance" any more: no source gives one. Distance counts through how hard the next shot is to make.

New in V3.19

  • Coaching plays position first: closer is better (about a foot from the next ball in the right place is best; very close or far away costs), and the leave counts more than before.
  • The cue ball stays on its side of the next shot's line: a plan that crosses it counts for less.
  • Softer when it can: the shape with a little side spin rather than a harder hit.
  • Cheating the pocket: the plan can say to hit the ball a little thinner or fuller for shape.

New in V3.18

  • Position closer to the next ball: a leave 4 or 5 feet away now counts for less, so the plan plays for shape instead of the easy shot with a long leave.
  • The zone is a long fan from the next ball along the planned line, coloured by how good the next shot is at each distance: you can see it get better nearer the ball and worse further out.
  • The perfect spot is the middle of the good stretch, so a little long or short still leaves you good; the plan picks the speed that stops there.

New in V3.17

  • A ball that went in too fast for the camera to see: the pocket is worked out from where it sat and where the shot was going, marked "⊘ not seen".
  • Shallow pockets: tap "Not a ball" on a ball lying in a pocket once, and that pocket is remembered for your table.
  • "Not a ball" on anything else stays removed for the session.
  • Your level on the home screen (Fargo, about): the coaching plays one level up, billiard theory for the next step, not your own game. The draw test no longer limits the plan.
  • A very hard speed for draws near break speed; "great position" when the cue ball lands in the green.

New in V3.16

  • Make percentages for a typical Fargo 550 instead of a much weaker player; a stroke with lots of side, a big draw or follow, or a hard hit costs some of its percentage.
  • No extra cost for going more cushions to come into the line.
  • Your cushions from your spin test: big side spin now comes off up to about 35 degrees, as on your table. Your earlier spin test is used, no need to redo it.
  • Draw test: a hard draw counts even when the object ball is in the pocket before the camera sees it move; draws are judged at draw speeds (soft draw at your medium, medium at your hard, hard near break speed).
  • A ball frozen on the cushion is planned as on the cushion (it was read a fraction into it, and the plan offered a kick).

New in V3.15

  • The plan plays for position with side spin and extra cushions when that comes into the line, not soft rolls that leave you far from the next ball.
  • Speeds are soft, soft to medium, medium, medium to hard and hard; side spin in tips ("1 tip left").
  • Menu in the Coaching test: Power test, Spin test (1 to 3 tips of left from the foot spot into the short cushion) and Draw test (soft, medium, hard draw and follow on a straight shot). The plan then uses your own speeds, spin and draw.
  • A cue ball moved into place is no longer counted as a test roll.
  • The debug file always has the session before, even when you went back to the home screen.

New in V3.14

  • V3.14.1: pockets marked with a dot and a short line out to the ball's number, instead of big circles. Band lines say OC (overcut) and UC (undercut). The shot lines are thinner.
  • The power test works: no roll is taken for a hand move any more. It asks for your soft, medium and hard, three rolls each.
  • The plan says soft, medium or hard instead of a number; after the power test, by your own speeds.
  • The zone is a fan round the perfect spot, seen from the next ball: wider on the side that still leaves a good shot, 30 degrees at most. Bright green at the spot, yellow edges, no outlines.
  • A line in the ball's colour from the object ball to its pocket, off the cushions for a bank.
  • A ball that only just lets the shot past is "tight", not blocked: the plan says "Tight past the 4". Fewer safeties when a pot is there.
  • After the shot: where the cue ball stopped against the spot, in inches long or short and to the side.

New in V3.13

  • Coaching test: a Reject plan button. Tap it when a plan makes no sense; it is saved with a picture of the table for the review.
  • The plan plays for position first: strokes that leave the cue ball in the zone, coming into the line. When no stroke can reach it, it says so.
  • The cue ball's path band: one line for hitting the ball thin, one for thick, see-through white between.
  • Stronger zone colours. Livelier cushion rebounds in the plan.

New in V3.12

  • Coaching test: there is always a plan. Banks, one- and two-rail kicks and safeties are planned when there is no good pot; the best plan first, the next best kind under it.
  • Percentages: each plan says its chance to make the ball and to have the next shot (a safety: the chance to hit it right and what it leaves). The zone's colours are percentages too: green 90%+, light green 80, yellow 60, red 50.

New in V3.11

  • Coaching test: the stroke is worked out in a ball-physics simulation (tip height, side spin, power, cushions, throw), tried with small mistakes, and simple strokes come first. A tough position says so.
  • The cue ball's path shows a faint band: where it goes if you cheat the pocket to the inside or the outside.
  • After a scratch: where to put the cue ball (ball in hand).
  • After each shot: how far the cue ball came off the planned line and how much further or shorter it went.
  • Menu → Power test: roll the cue ball alone at your power 2, 4, 6 and 8; the plan then shows its power in your numbers.

New in V3.10

  • Coaching test (above): small runouts with the 9 always on. Set out any balls and shoot the lowest first. Rings in each ball's colour show the pocket for the ball you're on, the next one and the one after. Green is where the cue ball should land for the next ball (red: the wrong side; dark: a ball in the way), with the cue ball's way there. After each shot it says where the cue ball landed.

New in V3.9

  • All fifteen balls: the 10 to 15 are read now, stripes seen end on too (a white cap with a coloured ring is not the cue ball). Practice reads all fifteen.
  • 8-ball: pick the game above, then Play a rack. The rack is over when the 8 goes in, or when only the cue ball is left.
  • A shot is told by your pose: down on the shot counts, standing at the table with a hand on a ball does not. Standing only keeps a move out when the app is sure.

New in V3.6

  • Drills (Menu → Drills): a cut ladder, thin cuts, a cross-side bank and a stop shot. Rings show where the balls go and the pocket; made, the next one is harder; missed, it steps back. Your drills over all sessions on this screen.
  • Free practice says what went in and how cut it was, and leaves the rest alone: a miss may have been a bank or anything.
  • In practice a cue ball picked up is not a scratch, and a row of balls along a rail is not a break.
  • Practice: set balls where you like, move them, pick them up. No racks; balls put back keep their numbers. Play a rack is for a proper rack.
  • Set the same shot up again and the review puts the tries together: all of them on one drawing, made or missed, with each try's stroke.
  • Picking up the cue ball to set a shot again is no longer called a scratch.
  • Balls put back on the table get their numbers again, even when no new rack was seen.
  • The stroke: the strike timed right on hard shots, and a small waggle is not counted as a practice stroke.
  • From V3.2: tap any shot after a session to see it from above with its pictures and clip; your stroke from the side, read on every shot.
